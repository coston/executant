## How to Use This Feedback

- **Revise the previous attempt; do not rebuild it from nothing.** It is the
  starting point. Re-gather only the context you need to address the feedback —
  rediscovering the rest from scratch is how a part of the output the judge
  never objected to silently regresses.
- **Preserve the original output structure** — match the same format, sections,
  and conventions as the original step requires; do not restructure or reframe
  the output unless the feedback explicitly asks you to.
- **Change only what the feedback asks for.** Address the specific concern
  raised and leave the rest alone. Do not discard an approach the judge did not
  object to, and never delete an honest statement — a caveat, a limitation,
  something you could not verify — to make the output look more complete. An
  accurate output that admits a gap beats a tidy one that invents its way past
  it, and the judge is not asking you to invent.
- **If the feedback is factually wrong, say so.** The judge sees only your text
  output, not your tool calls or their results, so it can mistake something you
  did for something you skipped. When you can check the claim and it does not
  hold, state plainly in your output what you actually did and what you
  verified, rather than changing correct work to match a mistaken note.

Now proceed with the step as originally described, incorporating the feedback above.
